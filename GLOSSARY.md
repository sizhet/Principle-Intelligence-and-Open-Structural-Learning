# 3. Principle Intelligence

## Definition

**Principle Intelligence** is the proposed intelligence paradigm in which reusable structural Principles are extracted from evidence, tested against supporting and counter-evidence, instantiated in context, revised through runtime experience, and potentially externalized as portable intelligence.

Canonical flow:

```text
Experience
    ↓
Difference
    ↓
Candidate Principle
    ↓
Validation
    ↓
Principle
    ↓
Context Binding
    ↓
Runtime
    ↓
Evidence
    ↓
Revision
```

## Key Distinction

Principle Intelligence is not merely:

```text
Rule Execution
```

and not merely:

```text
Statistical Prediction
```

Its distinctive concern is:

> **The formation, validation, reuse, and evolution of explicit reusable structural knowledge.**

---

# 4. Principle

## Definition

A **Principle** is:

> **A compact, reusable structural invariant, constraint, or relation inferred from multiple differences, transitions, counterfactuals, or critical evidence, intended to remain useful beyond the observations from which it was extracted.**

A Principle is not assumed to be universally true.

It is better treated as:

> **An evidence-bound, context-bound, falsifiable, and evolvable structural hypothesis.**

## Canonical Form

Conceptually:

```text
Principle P

Scope:
S

Context Requirements:
C

Structural Relation:
R

Supporting Evidence:
E+

Counter-Evidence:
E-

Exceptions:
X

Validation State:
V
```

## Important Distinction

```text
Principle
≠
Truth
```

and:

```text
Principle
≠
Rule
```

A Principle can generate multiple context-specific operational structures.

# 5. Candidate Principle

## Definition

A **Candidate Principle** is a structural hypothesis that has been extracted from evidence but has not yet accumulated sufficient validation for promotion into a more trusted reusable Knowledge Root.

Typical flow:

```text
Difference
    ↓
Structural Pattern
    ↓
Candidate Principle
    ↓
Supporting Evidence
+
Counter-Evidence Search
    ↓
Validation
```

## Key Property

Candidate status preserves uncertainty.

The architecture should not collapse:

```text
Interesting Pattern
```

directly into:

```text
Trusted Principle
```

---

# 6. Rule

## Definition

A **Rule** is an operational relation such as:

```text
IF Condition
THEN Action
```

or:

```text
Condition
→
Conclusion
```

Rules are generally more directly executable than Principles.

## Principle vs Rule

```text
Principle
    ↓
Context Binding
    ↓
Rule / CCC
```

A Principle may generate many Rules.

A Rule does not necessarily explain the reusable structural relation from which it was derived.

# 7. Principle as CCC Generator

## Definition

The phrase:

> **Principle is a CCC Generator**

means that a reusable Principle can be bound to different contexts to generate multiple context-specific CCC structures.

Example:

```text
Principle P
    ↓
Context A
    ↓
CCC-A
```

and:

```text
Principle P
    ↓
Context B
    ↓
CCC-B
```

Thus the Principle preserves reusable structure while the CCC carries contextual specialization.

# 8. Observation

## Definition

An **Observation** is an acquired description, measurement, event, state, transition, output, or other evidence-bearing representation produced through interaction with a system or environment.

An observation is not automatically a Principle.


# 9. Difference

## Definition

A **Difference** is a structurally meaningful distinction between observations, states, trajectories, expectations, predictions, contexts, outcomes, or intelligence structures.

Difference is broader than arithmetic subtraction.

Possible forms include:

```text
Euclidean Difference

Directional Difference

Temporal Difference

Behavioral Difference

Causal Difference

Metric Difference

Policy Difference

Context Difference

Trajectory Difference
```

## Key Proposition

> **Difference is an intelligence primitive.**

# 10. Differential Intelligence

## Definition

**Differential Intelligence** is intelligence obtained by explicitly identifying, representing, comparing, and exploiting meaningful differences.

Instead of asking only:

```text
What is X?
```

Differential Intelligence asks:

```text
How is X different from Y?

What changed?

Where did expectation fail?

What structural Delta explains the difference?
```

# 11. Differential Evidence

## Definition

**Differential Evidence** is evidence whose intelligence value arises primarily from the difference it reveals rather than from the isolated observation itself.

Example:

```text
Expected Outcome:
B

Observed Outcome:
C

Differential Evidence:
B ≠ C
```

The difference may reveal a missing structural condition.

---

# 12. Critical Differential Evidence

## Definition

**Critical Differential Evidence** is a Difference whose structural information value is disproportionately high relative to its frequency.

It may reveal:

```text
a hidden constraint

a false symmetry

a missing variable

a new direction

a context boundary

a new relation

a new structural dimension
```

Thus:

```text
Evidence Frequency
≠
Structural Importance
```

# 13. Small-Sample Structural Leverage

## Definition

**Small-Sample Structural Leverage** is the proposed increase in relative value of explicit structural information when statistical evidence is sparse.

Canonical proposition:

> **As evidence becomes sparse, the value of explicit structural leverage can increase.**

Compactly:

```text
Sample ↓
   ↓
Explicit Structural Leverage ↑
```

This does not imply that neural or statistical methods cannot operate with small samples.

It identifies a growing role for explicit:

```text
Difference

Constraint

Counter-Evidence

Transition

Invariant

Trigger
```

when evidence is limited.

# 14. Structural Residual

## Definition

A **Structural Residual** is the remaining unexplained difference between an expected structural relation and observed evidence.

Conceptually:

```text
Expected Structure
        ↓
Observed Structure
        ↓
Difference
        ↓
Structural Residual
```

A persistent residual may indicate:

```text
missing context

wrong metric

missing condition

incorrect Principle

new structural dimension
```

# 15. Delta

## Definition

A **Delta** is a localized structural difference that may explain why one state, behavior, context, or outcome differs from another.

Within this repository:

```text
Delta
```

is not restricted to numerical change.

It may be:

```text
Structural Delta

Context Delta

Policy Delta

Behavioral Delta

Metric Delta

Trajectory Delta

Principle Delta
```

---

# 16. Small Delta Intelligence

## Definition

**Small Delta Intelligence** is the idea that a relatively small structural difference may carry enough information to explain, repair, specialize, or grow a much larger intelligence structure.

Example:

```text
Large Existing Structure
        +
Small Critical Delta
        ↓
New Useful Structure
```

This is especially important in sparse-evidence settings.

# 17. Difference of Differences

## Definition

A **Difference of Differences** is a higher-order comparison between successive or related differences.

Conceptually:

```text
D1 = Difference(A, B)

D2 = Difference(B, C)

Difference-of-Differences
=
Difference(D1, D2)
```

It can expose:

```text
curvature

acceleration

change in relation

change in structural regime

trajectory bending
```

This concept is useful when the intelligence lies not merely in state change but in how the change itself changes.

# 20. Counter-Evidence

## Definition

**Counter-Evidence** is evidence that challenges the scope, conditions, interpretation, prediction, or structural validity of a Principle.

Counter-evidence does not automatically mean:


```text

wrong scope

```

# 21. Counter-Evidence Delta

## Definition

A **Counter-Evidence Delta** is the structural difference between contexts where a Principle is supported and contexts where it fails.

Canonical flow:

```text

      vs

      ↓
```

This is a major mechanism of Principle growth.

# 22. Delta-Minimal Revision

## Definition

**Delta-Minimal Revision** is the strategy of modifying a Principle by the smallest structural change that explains both previously supporting evidence and newly discovered counter-evidence.

Conceptually:

```text
Old Principle
+
Minimal Missing Delta
=
Revised Principle
```

This helps preserve useful prior structure while incorporating new evidence.



# 30. Evidence-Bound Intelligence

## Definition



# 31. Evidence Independence

## Definition

**Evidence Independence** concerns whether multiple pieces of supporting evidence provide genuinely independent support or merely repeat information derived from the same source.

This matters because:

```text
100 copies of one claim
≠
100 independent observations
```

Poor evidence independence can create false confidence.

# 65. Closed-Space Computation

## Definition

**Closed-Space Computation** is computation in which the relevant representational vocabulary, operators, state space, or structural object classes are substantially fixed before execution.

Canonical form:

```text
Defined Space
    ↓
Search / Optimize
    ↓
Answer Within Space
```

Closed-space computation is not inherently undesirable.

It is simply different from systems designed for structural vocabulary growth.

---

# 66. Open-Space Growth

## Definition

**Open-Space Growth** is the process by which an intelligence system expands the structural space in which future computation can occur.

Canonical form:

```text
Space V0
    ↓
Encounter Gap
    ↓
Create Structure X
    ↓
Space V1 = V0 + X
```

---

# 67. Structural Possibility Space

## Definition

The **Structural Possibility Space** is the set of structural objects, relations, compositions, questions, and operations that an intelligence system can meaningfully represent or construct.

When the structural vocabulary grows, the possibility space can also grow.

---

# 68. Open-Ended Structural Growth

## Definition

**Open-Ended Structural Growth** is continuing intelligence growth in which the system does not merely accumulate answers inside a fixed representational space but may develop new structures, relations, representations, and compositional possibilities.

Canonical proposition:

> **Growth is not merely adding answers to a fixed space. Growth can enlarge the space in which answers are possible.**

# 74. Transport ≠ Trust

## Definition

**Transport ≠ Trust** is the principle that successful transfer of an intelligence structure does not establish its correctness, suitability, safety, or contextual validity.

```text
limit

revise
```

the imported structure.

# 157. Final Terminology Principle

The terminology of this repository follows one general rule:

> **Do not name every intermediate artifact “knowledge.” Distinguish observation, difference, evidence, Principle, context-bound execution, portable structure, and collective revision according to their structural roles.**

This separation is important because each object answers a different question:

```text
Observation:
What happened?

Difference:
What changed?

Evidence:
What supports or challenges the structure?

Principle:
What may generalize?

CCC:
What does it mean here?

Two-Way CCC:
Does reality support the structural explanation?

Counter-Evidence:
Where does it fail?

Knowledge Root:
What reusable structure can generate further intelligence?

Open-LHS:
What structures may participate?

Runtime Structural Generation:
What missing structure must be created?

PIRP / PIRU:
What intelligence can travel?

Collective Learning:
How does one runtime's structural growth
become another runtime's starting point?

Open Structural Learning:
How can the structural language itself continue to grow?
```
