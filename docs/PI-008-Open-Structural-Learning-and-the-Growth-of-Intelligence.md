## Abstract

Many computational systems operate inside a possibility space defined before runtime.

The system may search that space extremely effectively.

It may optimize:

```text
parameters

actions

paths

tokens

policies

plans
```

but the representational objects, operators, interfaces, and structural vocabulary within which optimization occurs often remain largely predetermined.

Principle Intelligence suggests a broader form of learning.

Experience can reveal critical differences.

Differences can produce candidate invariants, constraints, and relations.

These can become Principles.

Principles can generate CCCs.

Counter-evidence can revise Principles.

Open-LHS reasoning can incorporate structures that were not present when the original reasoning system was designed.

Runtime Structural Generation can create missing intelligence objects.

PIRP/PIRU can externalize those objects.

UTN can make unfamiliar structures identifiable and structurally bindable.

Collective Learning can transport them between agents.

The result is a learning architecture in which the system may not only improve values inside an existing structural space, but may also extend the structural space available to future reasoning.

This document calls this paradigm:

> **Open Structural Learning**

Its central distinction is:

```text
Optimization:
Search within a defined possibility space.

Open Structural Learning:
Learn, create, validate, and reuse structures
that may extend the possibility space
available to future search.
```

This does not imply unlimited freedom, unconstrained self-modification, or escape from physical, logical, computational, or policy constraints.

The claim is narrower and more operational:

> **An intelligence system need not assume that all useful representational and computational objects must be fixed before learning begins.**

The system may discover new structural abstractions from experience, generate new intelligence objects when existing structures are insufficient, expose those objects through explicit interfaces, and allow them to participate in subsequent reasoning.

The deepest form of intelligence growth may therefore be not merely:

```text
better answers
```

but:

```text
new reusable structures
that make previously unavailable
answers, searches, and actions possible.
```

# 1. What Does It Mean for Intelligence to Grow?

Suppose a system solves a planning problem today with accuracy:

```text
70%
```

and tomorrow reaches:

```text
80%
```

The system has improved.

But has its intelligence structure grown?

Possibly.

Possibly not.

The improvement may have come from:

```text
more compute

more samples

better parameter estimation

longer search

better optimization
```

without changing the structural vocabulary available to the system.

This motivates a distinction between:

> **performance improvement**

and:

> **structural growth**

---

# 2. Performance Improvement

A system improves performance when:

```text
same task space
+
same representational structure
+
better parameter values
↓
better result
```

This is important and often sufficient.

But it represents only one form of intelligence improvement.

# 3. Structural Growth

A system exhibits structural growth when experience causes it to acquire a reusable structural object that did not previously exist in its usable intelligence repertoire.

For example:

```text

New Calling Graph

New PIRP
```

The important property is not merely novelty.

The new object becomes available for future computation.

# 5. Open Structural Learning

We define:

>
> **A learning paradigm in which reusable representational and computational structures may be discovered, generated, validated, revised, composed, and introduced into future reasoning rather than being restricted to a permanently closed structural vocabulary.**

The word **Open** refers specifically to the structural space.

# 13. Sample ↓ → Structural Leverage ↑

When evidence is abundant, statistical learning can estimate regularities effectively.

When evidence is sparse but structurally informative, explicit differences, constraints, counter-evidence, and causal transitions can become especially valuable.

A useful heuristic is:

> **Sample ↓ → Explicit Structural Leverage ↑**

This does not imply that neural learning stops working with small samples.

It means that explicit structural information may become disproportionately valuable as evidence becomes sparse.
